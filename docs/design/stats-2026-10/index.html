Stats: four concepts

Four ways for the app to show your training stats. Each one differs in what it leads with, where stats live, its main chart and how you interact with it. Each is a clickable prototype. Frame P on every board is live, and a switch on the board shows each data state (full history, 3 sessions, 1 session, fresh install). All four use the same 17 weeks of A/B data, generated by brief-data.py, so the numbers match across concepts.

Each concept was reviewed independently, revised, re-checked by the same reviewer, and given a last fix round. Scores below are from each concept's latest review, out of 10: look · gym context · IA · chart craft · data honesty · fidelity · distinctness.

Recommendation: a hybrid built on Verdict Feed

  • Home: Verdict Feed's verdict sentence and attention cards with Why?, plus Ladder's "12 of 14 lifts climbed a step" and a small Training Week adherence tile with the day strip.
  • Lift page: Progression Ladder's ladder: working weight as steps, reps-to-target bars beneath, deloads as hollow dots off the line.
  • Muscles: Training Week's bullet bars: done, still planned, and a plan tick.
  • Summary: Ladder's moves in words, plus "Next time".
  • Sparse data: Verdict Feed's expectation list on a fresh install, and no deltas before 4 sessions.

Runner-up: Progression Ladder as a whole. Main risk: the rules that classify lifts (moving / stalled / slipping, records). At some point during the run, every concept showed a sentence that contradicted its own data. Those rules need tests against this dataset before any UI work. Full reasoning: cross-concept review.

1 · Verdict Feed

Stats as written findings, ranked by what needs action.

Headline
"11 of 14 lifts moving. DB bench has stalled for 10 weeks." Counts, then stall, slip and ready cards.
Where
A Progress tab. Each card opens an evidence page, then the session.
Main chart
One lift over time, with labelled periods (Holiday, Deload, Stalled 10 wk).
Interaction
Tap Why? for the rule; a "4 weeks ago" reference; scrub.
Look
White, serif headlines (numbers always in the sans), claret only for attention.

2 · Lift Ledger

All 14 lifts in one table of sparklines on a shared time axis.

Sparkline rows; an expanded row shows weight, reps and e1RM panels.
Interaction

3 · Training Week

The week is the unit: are you doing the plan, and is it paying off?

Headline
2 / 3 sessions, "11 of 14 lifts moving", sets 47 of 69.
Where
A week pager with recaps; zooms out to an 8-week Block.
Main chart
Bullet graphs (done · still planned · plan tick); per lift, a strip binned by week.
Interaction
Step through weeks; compare with the last week of the same A/B pattern; drag to scrub.
Look
Dark graphite, calm azure and amber. No neon.

4 · Progression Ladder

No Stats tab: stats live at the lift, inside training.

Headline
"12 of 14 lifts climbed a step since 18 Sep", plus reps to the next weight.
Where
The Program list is the index (a mini ladder per lift), then a full-screen page per lift, then the summary.
Main chart
Weight steps over reps-to-target bars, with a target tick per weight.
Interaction
Readout docked in thumb reach; hold to compare two sessions.
Look
Results-board style: condensed tabular numbers, cobalt, calm.

Wrong in the app today, whichever concept you pick

These come from the audit, which ran the real app with 17 weeks of seeded data and recomputed every number:

  • Warm-up sets leak into the muscle panel (Workout B: rows sum to 22 against a header of 21).
  • Lats and Mid back are 13 and 9 on the Dashboard but merged into 21 on Sessions.
  • 446 records in 45 sessions, because "most reps at the heaviest weight" counts even when reps fell. The live strip and the summary count records differently.
  • Time axes are spaced by session, not date, so missed and sick weeks vanish. A 9-week bench plateau reads "+4.2 kg".
  • e1RM drops on a PR day (70×7 reads below 67.5×10). One weighted hyper-extension session wipes that lift's rep history from the chart.
  • The summary's "1 h 11 min" overlaps the Volume figure on every session over an hour.
  • Stats.tsx, HistoryStatsSwitch and ProgressionBar stopped being mounted after the redesign.
